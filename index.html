What it is, in one paragraph

The Seed is a handful of small files. Give it a model and an empty workspace, and it grows an organism of software one cell at a time: the model writes each cell, code checks it, and a cell is kept only if it bonds — proven down to an axiom the seed can re-derive itself. A cell that cannot prove itself dissolves. Nothing irreversible happens without a human. What grows is alive, because every part of it was checked.

Start here

The words this page uses. A cell is one unit of work the model does — a sum worked out, a figure cited. A cell bonds when a bond-check passes and the seed can re-derive that verdict itself at an axiom floor (exact arithmetic, or the source's own bytes); one that cannot bond dissolves and is not kept. The strength of a bond is how true it is — proven (exact), or grounded (as true as a document) — and a cell is only as strong as the weakest thing it stands on. The genome is four laws every cell runs on itself at birth: describe, verify, prune, know your edge. To plant is to give the seed a model and a workspace; it probes the floor (what the model gets right cold — on its own, before any checking), grows cells, and the pattern is self-similar — a sealed cell carries the seed, so it reproduces the next cell. The organism is alive when a cell both carries the seed and reproduces a sealed next cell. "Gated 13/13" in the blueprint means a mutation test changed that organ's code 13 ways, one small change at a time, and its tests caught every one.

How it works

One turn grows one cell. The model generates, code verifies or dissolves, and what survives crystallises:

task ──▶ MODEL generates a reply │ ▼ BOND-CHECK ──▶ does it reduce to an axiom floor the seed re-derives itself? │ │ no │ │ yes ▼ ▼ DISSOLVE GENOME birth-check: describe · verify · prune · know-edge (not kept) │ ▼ SEALED CELL ──▶ carries the seed ──▶ forges the next cell
  1. Generate. The model is given one task and writes one reply. The seed supplies no answer.
  2. Verify or dissolve. A verifier checks the reply, and the seed re-derives the same verdict at the axiom floor. If the verifier and the floor do not agree, nothing bonds. A verifier can never pass something the seed cannot ground itself.
  3. Crystallise. A cell seals only if it bonds and passes the genome's four laws — so every sealed cell is a cell, able to be planted again.
  4. Label. Each cell carries its strength, and no cell is ever called stronger than the weakest bond it stands on.

The two terminations make growth safe to grant. Below: nothing bonds that does not reduce to a recognised axiom floor. Above: anything irreversible — adopting new weights, publishing, deleting, spending, rewriting the floor — is refused unless a human hands in a one-time grant the seed has no way to issue for itself.

What this does not prove
  • It is a first cell, not a finished organism. The seed grows and seals real cells and proves the pattern reproduces. It does not claim to have grown the whole estate; it claims the loop is alive and every bond is checked.
  • Grounded is not true. A grounded cell cites a document faithfully. If the document is wrong, the answer is wrong, and it is still grounded. The label is the promise, not a guarantee of the world.
  • The floor is small on purpose. Two axiom floors ship — exact arithmetic and byte-equality. The seed carries the contract any other verifier plugs into (with its own floor and honest strength); it does not carry the whole estate's verifiers. That is what "minimal and transmissible" means.
  • The seed does not train. Its distillation organ harvests verified cells and gates adoption; it changes no weights, and it never adopts without beating the sealed set and a human grant. The training itself is a separate build, The Distillation.
  • The model is not pinned. The browser model comes from the public WebLLM library; this page does not check it against a fixed fingerprint, so a changed file upstream would not be caught here.
For buyers

Most "autonomous" AI is a loop with nothing checking it: it generates, and you hope. The expensive failure is the confident output nobody caught. The Seed is the opposite shape — a growth loop where every cell is checked against something you can re-derive yourself, kept only if it proves out, and labelled exactly how true it is.

  • For the board. The seed terminates at both ends: below, nothing is kept that it cannot re-derive from first principles itself; above, nothing irreversible — adopting weights, publishing, deleting, spending — happens unless a person hands in a one-time grant the seed has no way to issue for itself. A system that cannot persist an unverified result, and cannot act irreversibly without a human, is one you can grant real autonomy without granting real risk.
  • For engineering. It is small, pure, and transmissible — a handful of files you plant in your own workspace with your own model. Plug in your own verifiers through one contract; each declares its floor and its strength.
  • For security and ownership. It runs on your hardware. The model and the work stay on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the published runs are re-runnable, so you check the claims yourself rather than trust ours.

White paper: a seed, not a program

The problem. A program does one job and stops. An AI left to grow on its own does not stop, and nothing tells it which of its own steps was wrong, so it compounds its mistakes as readily as its successes. Neither is what you want from a system meant to build.

The idea. Carry the whole pattern of a verified, self-growing organism in the smallest block that still holds it, and make every fusion verify-or-dissolve. The model generates; code verifies down to an axiom the seed re-derives itself; what proves out crystallises into a cell; the cell carries the seed and reproduces. Growth adds up instead of washing out, because nothing unverified is ever kept.

How it is trusted. Every organ is pure and total — a hostile input returns a refusal, never a throw — and every organ is mutation-gated: its tests must catch every small change to its code. The whole thing terminates at both ends: an axiom floor below that nothing bonds without reducing to, and a person above that no irreversible act passes without.

What this page tests. That a small model, planted in a browser tab with this seed, probes a floor, grows and seals real cells of two strengths, and reproduces the pattern — a first cell, alive — with no number written down that a recorded, re-runnable plant did not measure.

What it does not show. A finished organism. The seed is honest about scale: the loop is alive and every bond is checked; the estate grows from here, cell by cell, each one sealed before the next.

The one rule. Verify or dissolve. Nothing persists that cannot prove itself, and nothing is called truer than what it stands on.
